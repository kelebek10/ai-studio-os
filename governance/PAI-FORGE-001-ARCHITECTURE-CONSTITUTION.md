---

# 1. PURPOSE

PAI-FORGE is the foundational architecture and intelligence infrastructure for the development of a proprietary, evidence-grounded, model-agnostic landscape intelligence system.

The system is designed to combine:

- scientific knowledge
- structured environmental data
- geographic data
- plant data
- verified algorithms
- engineering rules
- field knowledge
- project data
- evidence
- deterministic calculations
- AI-assisted reasoning
- visual intelligence
- decision support

into a controlled and continuously improving intelligence platform.

PAI-FORGE is not defined by any individual AI model.

Its permanent value shall reside in:

- validated data
- knowledge structures
- decision rules
- scientific evidence
- provenance
- algorithms
- project history
- evaluation systems
- domain expertise
- proprietary processes

AI models are replaceable execution and reasoning components.

---

# 2. PRIMARY OBJECTIVE

The primary objective of PAI-FORGE is to create a reliable intelligence foundation capable of supporting professional landscape design, analysis, planning, implementation, irrigation, plant selection, project management, cost estimation and future landscape-related applications.

The system shall prioritize:

1. correctness
2. evidence
3. safety
4. determinism
5. reproducibility
6. traceability
7. scalability
8. maintainability
9. commercial value
10. user experience

Speed shall never override correctness or system integrity.

---

# 3. SYSTEM IDENTITY

PAI-FORGE is the internal intelligence and architecture platform.

It is not required to be the public-facing brand.

Public-facing products may use different names, interfaces or brands while consuming services from PAI-FORGE.

PAI-FORGE shall therefore remain:

- product-independent
- model-independent
- interface-independent
- vendor-independent
- deployment-independent

where technically and commercially practical.

---

# 4. CORE PRINCIPLE

The permanent intellectual value of the system shall be stored inside the controlled PAI-FORGE Core rather than inside an external AI provider.

No external AI provider shall become the sole repository of:

- proprietary knowledge
- validated rules
- project intelligence
- decision history
- proprietary datasets
- business-critical logic

AI providers are replaceable.

PAI-FORGE Core is not.

---

# 5. ARCHITECTURAL PHILOSOPHY

The primary reasoning architecture shall follow:

STRUCTURED-FIRST
        ↓
SEMANTIC-SECOND
        ↓
LLM-LAST

Structured data and deterministic rules shall be consulted before semantic retrieval.

Semantic retrieval shall be used where structured information is insufficient.

LLMs shall operate primarily as reasoning, interpretation, orchestration and communication layers rather than as the unquestioned source of truth.

---

# 6. DETERMINISTIC-FIRST PRINCIPLE

Whenever a problem can be solved reliably through:

- mathematics
- scientific equations
- validated thresholds
- structured rules
- database queries
- GIS calculations
- engineering formulas
- deterministic algorithms

the deterministic method shall take precedence over generative reasoning.

Examples include:

- evapotranspiration
- irrigation calculations
- soil parameter calculations
- spatial measurements
- plant hard constraints
- coordinate transformations
- area calculations
- slope calculations
- quantity calculations
- cost arithmetic

An LLM shall not replace a deterministic calculation when a validated deterministic method exists.

---

# 7. EVIDENCE-GROUNDED PRINCIPLE

Every important scientific, architectural, engineering or commercial decision shall be traceable to evidence.

Evidence may include:

- scientific publications
- official datasets
- government sources
- institutional documentation
- verified open-source repositories
- official APIs
- field measurements
- project records
- expert validation
- controlled experiments

Unsupported claims shall not be promoted to authoritative Core knowledge.

---

# 8. SOURCE HIERARCHY

PAI-FORGE shall prefer sources according to the following hierarchy:

1. direct measurement
2. official government or institutional data
3. peer-reviewed scientific literature
4. official dataset documentation
5. authoritative technical documentation
6. verified open-source implementation
7. expert-validated field knowledge
8. secondary technical sources
9. general web sources
10. unverified user-generated information

Lower-ranked sources may be useful for discovery but shall not automatically become authoritative evidence.

---

# 9. EXTERNAL INPUT BOUNDARY

All information entering PAI-FORGE from external sources shall initially be considered UNTRUSTED INPUT.

This includes:

- web research
- GitHub repositories
- downloaded datasets
- APIs
- uploaded documents
- external AI outputs
- generated code
- generated datasets
- third-party models

External information shall pass through an appropriate validation pipeline before entering the trusted Core.

No external research agent may directly write authoritative information into Core.

---

# 10. RESEARCH → EVIDENCE → KNOWLEDGE PIPELINE

The canonical information flow is:

DISCOVERY
   ↓
RAW RESEARCH
   ↓
SCREENING
   ↓
SOURCE VALIDATION
   ↓
EVIDENCE EXTRACTION
   ↓
CROSS-VERIFICATION
   ↓
APPROVAL
   ↓
CORE KNOWLEDGE
   ↓
DECISION RULES / DATA / ALGORITHMS
   ↓
APPLICATION

This pipeline shall be enforced through repository structure and governance.

---

# 11. AI MODEL PRINCIPLE

PAI-FORGE shall remain model-agnostic.

Supported AI models may include:

- ChatGPT
- Claude
- Gemini
- Kimi
- future models

No architecture decision shall assume that a particular AI provider will exist permanently.

AI models may be replaced, upgraded, removed or added without requiring reconstruction of the Core.

---

# 12. AI ROLE PRINCIPLE

AI models shall operate as specialized workers within a controlled system.

An AI model shall not be treated as the owner of the project.

AI responsibilities may include:

- research
- architecture analysis
- coding
- review
- testing
- scientific interpretation
- data extraction
- visual analysis
- documentation
- orchestration

Roles may change as the project develops.

The permanent authority remains the PAI-FORGE governance layer.

---

# 13. INDEPENDENT DISCOVERY PRINCIPLE

When multiple AI systems are assigned the same research mission, they should initially work independently.

Independent research is preferred because it reduces:

- groupthink
- early anchoring
- model-specific bias
- confirmation bias
- premature consensus

Each AI may use its own:

- search strategy
- reasoning method
- coding capability
- research tools
- technical expertise

Results shall subsequently enter the common evidence pipeline.

---

# 14. COMMON EVIDENCE POOL

Independent AI research shall converge into a common evidence pool.

The common pool shall preserve:

- source identity
- source URL
- source type
- publication date
- retrieval date
- license
- evidence strength
- claims extracted
- supporting references
- conflicts
- validation status
- responsible research agent

The common pool is not automatically the Core.

It is an intermediate evidence layer.

---

# 15. CONSENSUS PRINCIPLE

AI consensus alone shall not establish truth.

If:

- ChatGPT agrees
- Claude agrees
- Gemini agrees
- Kimi agrees

the result may receive increased confidence, but consensus does not replace evidence.

A single authoritative source may outweigh four unsupported AI opinions.

The decision process shall therefore be:

EVIDENCE
+
VALIDATION
+
CROSS-CHECK
+
TECHNICAL REVIEW
+
AI ANALYSIS
=
DECISION

---

# 16. HUMAN AUTHORITY

Human oversight remains mandatory for high-impact decisions.

Human approval may be required for:

- Core architecture changes
- security policies
- proprietary data policies
- production deployment
- financial commitments
- commercial licensing decisions
- irreversible data migrations
- major algorithm changes
- public release of proprietary knowledge

AI systems may recommend.

Authorized humans approve.

---

# 17. DECISION AUTHORITY

PAI-FORGE decisions shall follow this hierarchy:

LEVEL 1 — Evidence
LEVEL 2 — Scientific / technical validation
LEVEL 3 — Architecture rules
LEVEL 4 — Decision policy
LEVEL 5 — AI recommendation
LEVEL 6 — Human approval where required

An AI recommendation cannot override a higher-level governance rule.

---

# 18. MODEL OUTPUT TRUST LEVEL

AI-generated content shall have an explicit trust status where appropriate.

Suggested statuses:

UNVERIFIED
DISCOVERED
SCREENED
EVIDENCE-BACKED
CROSS-VERIFIED
APPROVED
CORE
DEPRECATED
REJECTED

AI-generated statements shall not be treated as CORE solely because they are confidently written.

---

# 19. PROVENANCE PRINCIPLE

Important information must remain traceable to its origin.

For every authoritative data item or decision where practical, PAI-FORGE should be able to answer:

- Where did it originate?
- Who or what produced it?
- When was it obtained?
- Which source supports it?
- Was it transformed?
- Which validation was performed?
- Who approved it?
- Which version is currently active?

Loss of provenance shall reduce trust.

---

# 20. REPRODUCIBILITY PRINCIPLE

A major calculation or decision should be reproducible from its recorded inputs, rules, algorithms and source versions.

The system should avoid decisions that depend exclusively on hidden model intuition.

Where practical:

same input
+
same dataset version
+
same rule version
+
same algorithm version

should produce the same deterministic result.

---

# 21. OPEN SOURCE PRINCIPLE

PAI-FORGE may use open-source software, datasets and algorithms where their licenses permit the intended use.

Open-source adoption does NOT mean that PAI-FORGE itself becomes open source.

Third-party components shall remain separated from proprietary PAI-FORGE intellectual property where legally and technically appropriate.

Every dependency shall be evaluated for:

- license
- commercial rights
- attribution
- copyleft obligations
- SaaS implications
- redistribution requirements
- security
- maintenance
- dependency risk

---

# 22. PROPRIETARY CORE PRINCIPLE

The following categories are considered potentially proprietary PAI-FORGE assets:

- curated datasets
- regional landscape knowledge
- validated plant matrices
- proprietary decision rules
- regional coefficients
- project records
- field observations
- internal evaluation datasets
- proprietary algorithms
- business logic
- workflow definitions
- validated prompts and orchestration logic
- provenance records
- system-specific optimizations

These assets shall not be automatically published merely because an underlying open-source component is used.

---

# 23. DEPENDENCY ISOLATION

External software shall be treated as replaceable dependencies.

PAI-FORGE shall avoid unnecessary architectural coupling to a single:

- AI provider
- vector database
- cloud provider
- GIS provider
- API provider
- operating system
- visualization framework

Adapters and interfaces should be preferred where they materially reduce vendor lock-in.

---

# 24. DATA OWNERSHIP AND CONTROL

PAI-FORGE shall distinguish between:

1. public data
2. licensed third-party data
3. transformed third-party data
4. user-provided data
5. field-measured data
6. internally generated data
7. proprietary derived knowledge

Each category shall have its own access and usage policy.

Data shall not be copied into Core merely because it is technically accessible.

Legal and license constraints shall be part of data ingestion.

---

# 25. SECURITY-FIRST DATA INGESTION

External data ingestion shall consider:

- malicious files
- prompt injection
- hidden instructions
- poisoned datasets
- malicious code
- embedded secrets
- personal information
- credential leakage
- unsafe dependencies
- suspicious URLs

Research agents must not execute untrusted code merely because it appears in a repository or document.

Untrusted material must remain isolated until screened.

---

# 26. CORE BOUNDARY

The Core is the trusted intellectual layer of PAI-FORGE.

Core components may include:

- knowledge
- validated data
- rules
- algorithms
- provenance
- decisions
- evaluation records

No research agent shall bypass the Core governance boundary.

No application shall directly modify authoritative Core data without an approved data workflow.

---

# 27. ARCHITECTURAL LAYERS

PAI-FORGE shall conceptually maintain the following layers:

LAYER 1 — GOVERNANCE
Rules, policies, authority and standards.

LAYER 2 — RESEARCH
Discovery and investigation.

LAYER 3 — EVIDENCE
Validated external knowledge.

LAYER 4 — CORE
Trusted internal knowledge and decision intelligence.

LAYER 5 — SERVICES
APIs, calculation services and intelligence services.

LAYER 6 — APPLICATIONS
Web, mobile and user-facing interfaces.

LAYER 7 — INFRASTRUCTURE
Databases, storage, compute, deployment and security.

No lower layer should silently redefine the authority of a higher layer.

---

# 28. STRUCTURED DATA PRINCIPLE

Where information can be represented reliably as structured data, structured representation shall be preferred.

Examples:

- plant species
- taxonomy
- soil parameters
- climate variables
- irrigation parameters
- geographic coordinates
- project quantities
- prices
- measurements
- constraints
- suitability results

Narrative text may supplement structured data but should not unnecessarily replace it.

---

# 29. SEMANTIC LAYER PRINCIPLE

Semantic retrieval shall be used where exact structured queries are insufficient.

Examples include:

- scientific literature retrieval
- contextual knowledge retrieval
- similar project discovery
- document search
- evidence matching
- natural-language knowledge access

Semantic search shall not silently override deterministic facts.

---

# 30. LLM LAST PRINCIPLE

The LLM should normally receive validated context rather than independently inventing the underlying facts.

Preferred flow:

USER INPUT
↓
STRUCTURED DATA
↓
DETERMINISTIC RULES
↓
SEMANTIC RETRIEVAL
↓
VALIDATED CONTEXT
↓
LLM REASONING
↓
USER-FACING EXPLANATION

The LLM is therefore primarily an interpretation and reasoning layer.

---

# 31. FAILURE PRINCIPLE

When required information is unavailable or unreliable, the system shall be allowed to return:

UNKNOWN
INSUFFICIENT DATA
REQUIRES MEASUREMENT
REQUIRES VALIDATION

The system must not fabricate certainty merely to produce an answer.

---

# 32. UNCERTAINTY PRINCIPLE

Important environmental and project parameters shall be capable of carrying uncertainty or status metadata.

Examples include:

- KNOWN
- UNKNOWN
- ESTIMATED
- USER-PROVIDED
- MEASURED
- VISION-INFERRED
- MODEL-DERIVED
- VALIDATED

A numerical value without provenance may be insufficient for a high-confidence decision.

---

# 33. VERSIONING PRINCIPLE

Important artifacts shall be versioned.

This includes:

- architecture documents
- rules
- datasets
- algorithms
- schemas
- prompts
- evaluation sets
- model configurations
- decision policies

Changes must be traceable.

Historical versions shall not be silently overwritten when they affect reproducibility.

---

# 34. CHANGE CONTROL

Major architectural changes shall require:

1. reason for change
2. impact analysis
3. affected components
4. compatibility analysis
5. migration requirements
6. security implications
7. rollback strategy
8. approval status

The system shall prefer controlled evolution over uncontrolled modification.

---

# 35. MODULARITY PRINCIPLE

Every major subsystem should be:

- independently understandable
- independently testable
- independently replaceable where practical
- documented
- versioned

Modules must communicate through defined interfaces.

Hidden coupling shall be minimized.

---

# 36. SCALABILITY PRINCIPLE

PAI-FORGE shall initially favor simplicity and correctness.

However, architectural decisions should avoid blocking future expansion toward:

- larger datasets
- additional regions
- additional AI models
- additional applications
- additional users
- private infrastructure
- dedicated servers
- distributed computation
- international deployment

Premature complexity shall be avoided.

# 37. AI ORCHESTRATION PRINCIPLE

PAI-FORGE shall use AI models as coordinated, replaceable intelligence workers operating under defined governance rules.

AI orchestration shall assign tasks according to:

- capability
- reliability
- domain suitability
- cost
- latency
- security requirements
- evidence requirements
- task complexity

No AI model shall automatically become the permanent primary model.

The orchestration layer shall remain independent from individual model providers wherever technically practical.

---

# 38. SPECIALIZED AI WORKER PRINCIPLE

Different AI systems may be assigned specialized responsibilities according to their demonstrated capabilities.

Potential responsibilities include:

- research
- scientific analysis
- architecture
- software engineering
- code review
- security analysis
- testing
- data extraction
- visual analysis
- documentation
- adversarial review
- quality assurance

Assignments may change as model capabilities change.

The architecture shall not permanently encode assumptions that one AI is superior for every task.

---

# 39. AI INDEPENDENCE AND CROSS-REVIEW

When a decision is sufficiently important, independent AI analysis should be preferred before consensus formation.

Where appropriate:

AI-A
↓
AI-B
↓
AI-C
↓
INDEPENDENT RESULTS
↓
EVIDENCE COMPARISON
↓
CROSS-REVIEW
↓
DECISION PROPOSAL

AI systems should be capable of challenging conclusions produced by other AI systems.

Agreement is not itself proof of correctness.

---

# 40. AI OUTPUT BOUNDARY

AI-generated output shall remain outside trusted Core status until it passes the required validation process.

AI output may be classified as:

- research
- hypothesis
- recommendation
- draft
- evidence candidate
- validated result
- approved knowledge

The classification shall determine what downstream systems are permitted to do with the information.

No AI-generated output shall automatically modify authoritative:

- data
- rules
- algorithms
- schemas
- architecture
- security policies

without an approved workflow.

---

# 41. COMMON RESEARCH AND EVIDENCE POOL

PAI-FORGE shall maintain a controlled common pool through which independent research outputs may converge.

The pool should preserve:

- source
- claim
- evidence
- confidence
- provenance
- conflicts
- validation status
- research agent
- timestamp
- version
- licensing information

The evidence pool is a staging and validation environment.

It shall not be treated as equivalent to trusted Core knowledge.

---

# 42. EVIDENCE CONFLICT PRINCIPLE

When credible sources disagree, PAI-FORGE shall not silently select one result merely because it is convenient.

The system should record:

- conflicting claims
- source authority
- evidence quality
- publication date
- measurement conditions
- geographic context
- methodological differences
- unresolved uncertainty

Where necessary, the result shall remain:

CONFLICTED
or
REQUIRES VALIDATION

until an appropriate resolution is established.

---

# 43. KNOWLEDGE PROMOTION PRINCIPLE

Information may progress through controlled trust stages:

DISCOVERED
↓
SCREENED
↓
EVIDENCE-BACKED
↓
CROSS-VERIFIED
↓
APPROVED
↓
CORE

Promotion between stages shall require defined validation criteria.

Knowledge shall never become authoritative merely because it has been stored in a database or repository.

---

# 44. CORE KNOWLEDGE PRINCIPLE

Trusted Core knowledge shall consist only of information that has passed the applicable governance and validation requirements.

Core knowledge may include:

- validated scientific facts
- validated regional data
- approved plant information
- validated environmental parameters
- approved decision rules
- deterministic algorithms
- verified project records
- approved evaluation datasets
- provenance records

Core knowledge shall remain traceable, versioned and controlled.

---

# 45. CORE WRITE AUTHORITY

Write access to authoritative Core data shall be explicitly controlled.

Potential write operations include:

- CREATE
- UPDATE
- DEPRECATE
- REJECT
- RESTORE
- MIGRATE

Each operation affecting authoritative knowledge should preserve:

- actor
- timestamp
- reason
- previous version
- new version
- evidence
- approval status

Applications and research agents shall not receive unrestricted Core write access.

---

# 46. CORE VALIDATION GATE

Before information becomes authoritative Core knowledge, the system should verify, where applicable:

1. provenance
2. source quality
3. evidence strength
4. consistency
5. licensing
6. security
7. domain validity
8. regional applicability
9. reproducibility
10. approval status

If required validation cannot be completed, the information shall remain outside authoritative Core status.

---

# 47. CORE INTEGRITY AND CONTROL

The PAI-FORGE Core shall be protected against uncontrolled modification.

Core changes shall be:

- authorized
- versioned
- traceable
- reviewable
- reproducible where applicable
- reversible where practical

The Core may evolve as validated knowledge improves.

However, no research agent, external AI model or application may silently modify authoritative Core knowledge.

Changes to Core rules, schemas, algorithms or high-impact knowledge shall follow the applicable change-control and approval process.

---

# 48. CORE AND APPLICATION SEPARATION

Applications shall consume trusted Core services through defined interfaces.

Applications should not directly implement proprietary Core decision logic when that logic can be maintained centrally.

Preferred architecture:

APPLICATION
↓
SERVICE / API
↓
CORE KNOWLEDGE + RULES + ALGORITHMS
↓
RESULT

This separation shall protect consistency across multiple applications.

---

# 49. CORE AND AI SEPARATION

AI models shall consume authorized Core information through controlled interfaces.

Preferred flow:

CORE
↓
AUTHORIZED CONTEXT
↓
AI REASONING
↓
VALIDATED OUTPUT
↓
APPLICATION

AI models shall not become the permanent storage location for Core knowledge.

Model context, prompts and temporary reasoning artifacts shall be treated separately from authoritative knowledge.

---

# 50. KNOWLEDGE FEEDBACK LOOP

PAI-FORGE shall support a controlled learning loop:

FIELD / PROJECT
↓
OBSERVATION
↓
DATA CAPTURE
↓
VALIDATION
↓
EVIDENCE
↓
EVALUATION
↓
KNOWLEDGE UPDATE
↓
RULE / ALGORITHM IMPROVEMENT
↓
FUTURE PROJECT

The system shall distinguish between:

- observation
- interpretation
- validated knowledge
- derived rule

Field experience may improve the system, but individual observations shall not automatically become universal rules.

---

# 51. OPEN-SOURCE DISCOVERY AND ADOPTION BOUNDARY

GitHub repositories and other open-source resources may be used as discovery, reference, testing or implementation sources.

Discovery shall not automatically result in adoption.

Every external component shall pass the applicable:

- license review
- security review
- technical review
- dependency review
- maintenance assessment
- compatibility assessment
- commercial-use assessment

before becoming an approved dependency.

---

# 52. EXTERNAL CODE EXECUTION PRINCIPLE

Code obtained from external repositories, AI-generated sources or downloaded artifacts shall be considered untrusted until appropriately reviewed.

PAI-FORGE shall not execute external code merely because:

- it is popular
- it has many stars
- an AI recommends it
- it appears in a trusted repository
- it claims to be safe

Execution shall occur only within an appropriately controlled environment.

---

# 53. THIRD-PARTY COMPONENT ISOLATION

External libraries, models, datasets and services shall remain identifiable as third-party components.

Where practical, PAI-FORGE shall maintain:

- dependency manifests
- version records
- license records
- source references
- security status
- approval status
- replacement strategy

Third-party components shall not silently become indistinguishable from proprietary PAI-FORGE assets.

---

# 54. SECURITY-FIRST ARCHITECTURE

Security shall be incorporated into every architectural layer.

Security considerations shall include:

- identity
- authentication
- authorization
- secrets management
- data protection
- network security
- dependency security
- code execution
- logging
- monitoring
- backup
- recovery
- incident response

Security controls shall evolve with system capability.

---

# 55. UNTRUSTED CONTENT PRINCIPLE

External content shall be treated as data to be analyzed rather than instructions to be obeyed.

This includes:

- web pages
- documents
- repositories
- datasets
- API responses
- AI outputs
- code comments
- embedded metadata
- generated files

Instructions contained inside untrusted material shall not automatically acquire authority over PAI-FORGE.

---

# 56. PROMPT INJECTION DEFENSE

PAI-FORGE research and ingestion systems shall assume that external content may contain prompt injection or instruction manipulation.

External text shall not be allowed to override:

- system rules
- governance policies
- security controls
- data permissions
- Core authority
- user authorization boundaries

Prompt injection shall be treated as a security problem, not merely a model-quality problem.

---

# 57. SECRET AND PERSONAL DATA PROTECTION

PAI-FORGE shall prevent accidental ingestion, storage or publication of:

- API keys
- passwords
- access tokens
- private credentials
- private encryption material
- unnecessary personal information
- confidential customer information

Secret scanning and appropriate data classification should be applied to relevant ingestion and repository workflows.

Detected secrets shall be treated as security incidents where appropriate.

---

# 58. DATA CLASSIFICATION PRINCIPLE

PAI-FORGE data shall be classified according to its ownership, sensitivity and permitted use.

Possible classifications include:

- PUBLIC
- LICENSED
- INTERNAL
- CONFIDENTIAL
- PROPRIETARY
- RESTRICTED

Classification shall influence:

- storage
- access
- processing
- sharing
- retention
- publication

---

# 59. DETERMINISTIC DECISION ENGINE

Where validated rules and deterministic calculations exist, the decision engine shall execute them before invoking generative reasoning.

Preferred flow:

INPUT
↓
VALIDATION
↓
STRUCTURED FACTS
↓
DETERMINISTIC RULES
↓
CALCULATION
↓
RESULT
↓
AI INTERPRETATION

The AI layer may explain a deterministic result but shall not silently alter it.

---

# 60. DECISION TRACE PRINCIPLE

Important system decisions should preserve a decision trace containing, where applicable:

- input data
- data versions
- rules used
- algorithm versions
- evidence
- assumptions
- uncertainty
- intermediate results
- final result
- AI interpretation
- approval status

A decision without sufficient traceability shall have reduced confidence.

---

# 61. ABSTENTION PRINCIPLE

PAI-FORGE shall be capable of refusing to produce a high-confidence recommendation when required information is insufficient.

The system may return:

- UNKNOWN
- INSUFFICIENT DATA
- CONFLICTING EVIDENCE
- REQUIRES MEASUREMENT
- REQUIRES HUMAN REVIEW
- REQUIRES VALIDATION

Abstention is a valid system outcome.

The system shall prefer an explicit limitation over fabricated certainty.

---

# 62. TESTING PRINCIPLE

Every critical component shall be tested at an appropriate level.

Testing may include:

- unit testing
- integration testing
- schema validation
- deterministic calculation testing
- security testing
- regression testing
- adversarial testing
- model evaluation
- end-to-end testing
- field validation

Critical changes shall not be considered complete merely because the code executes successfully.

---

# 63. DETERMINISTIC CALCULATION TESTING

Deterministic engines shall have explicit test cases covering:

- normal inputs
- boundary conditions
- invalid inputs
- missing inputs
- extreme values
- unit conversions
- expected outputs
- known failure cases

Reference results should be maintained for critical calculations.

Changes to calculation logic shall trigger appropriate regression testing.

---

# 64. DATA AND SCHEMA TESTING

Important datasets and schemas shall be validated for:

- required fields
- data types
- ranges
- enumerations
- referential integrity
- duplicate records
- missing values
- invalid relationships
- provenance
- version compatibility

Schema changes shall not silently invalidate historical records.

---

# 65. AI MODEL EVALUATION

AI models used by PAI-FORGE shall be evaluated against task-specific criteria.

Evaluation may include:

- factual accuracy
- evidence adherence
- instruction adherence
- hallucination rate
- consistency
- reasoning quality
- structured output validity
- security behavior
- latency
- cost
- domain performance

Model selection shall be based on demonstrated capability rather than reputation alone.

---

# 66. MODEL REPLACEMENT TESTING

When an AI model is replaced or upgraded, the replacement shall be evaluated against the relevant existing evaluation set.

Where appropriate:

CURRENT MODEL
↓
BASELINE EVALUATION
↓
NEW MODEL
↓
COMPARATIVE EVALUATION
↓
REGRESSION CHECK
↓
APPROVAL
↓
DEPLOYMENT

Model replacement shall not silently change critical system behavior.

---

# 67. PRIVATE INFRASTRUCTURE READINESS

PAI-FORGE shall remain capable of future deployment on infrastructure controlled by the project where technically and commercially justified.

Future infrastructure may include:

- dedicated servers
- private cloud
- on-premises infrastructure
- private databases
- private object storage
- private GPU infrastructure
- local AI inference

The architecture should avoid unnecessary dependencies that would make future migration impractical.

---

# 68. INFRASTRUCTURE INDEPENDENCE

Infrastructure components shall be replaceable where practical.

PAI-FORGE should avoid permanent architectural dependence on a single:

- cloud provider
- database provider
- storage provider
- GPU provider
- hosting provider
- deployment platform

Infrastructure abstraction should be used where it provides meaningful long-term value.

---

# 69. DATA PORTABILITY

Core data should remain exportable in controlled, documented formats.

The system should support migration without losing:

- provenance
- identifiers
- relationships
- versions
- validation status
- licensing metadata
- decision history

Data portability is a strategic protection against vendor lock-in.

---

# 70. DISASTER RECOVERY AND BACKUP

Critical PAI-FORGE assets shall have appropriate backup and recovery mechanisms.

Backup strategy should consider:

- frequency
- retention
- encryption
- geographic redundancy where appropriate
- integrity verification
- restoration testing
- disaster scenarios
- recovery objectives

A backup that has never been tested for restoration shall not be assumed reliable.
