# PAI-FORGE-001 — ARCHITECTURE CONSTITUTION

Document ID: PAI-FORGE-001
Version: 1.0
Status: FOUNDATION
Authority: Human Project Owner

## 1. PURPOSE

PAI-FORGE is a private, modular, evidence-grounded Landscape Intelligence system.

Its permanent value shall reside in its own:

- validated data
- evidence
- domain knowledge
- decision rules
- deterministic calculation engines
- project records
- evaluation systems
- proprietary workflows

AI models are replaceable execution and reasoning components.

## 2. ARCHITECTURAL PRINCIPLE

PAI-FORGE follows:

STRUCTURED-FIRST
→ SEMANTIC-SECOND
→ LLM-LAST

If a problem can be solved reliably with structured data, mathematics, rules or deterministic computation, those methods have priority over LLM reasoning.

## 3. SYSTEM OWNERSHIP

PAI-FORGE Core, validated knowledge, proprietary datasets, decision rules and project intelligence are controlled by the project.

External:

- AI models
- GitHub repositories
- open-source libraries
- datasets
- APIs
- cloud services

are dependencies or sources, not the proprietary core.

## 4. MODEL-AGNOSTIC DESIGN

No critical capability shall permanently depend on one AI model or provider.

Claude, GPT, Gemini, Kimi, local models and future models may be used according to capability.

AI output is never automatically equivalent to system truth.

## 5. EVIDENCE-FIRST KNOWLEDGE

All externally obtained information is initially treated as:

UNTRUSTED EXTERNAL INPUT

Before entering trusted knowledge, it requires appropriate:

- source identification
- evidence validation
- cross-checking
- license verification
- security assessment
- provenance
- confidence assessment

## 6. DETERMINISTIC CORE

Scientific, spatial and operational calculations should use validated deterministic methods whenever available.

Examples:

- plant suitability
- environmental constraints
- irrigation
- evapotranspiration
- spatial analysis
- quantities
- cost calculations

LLMs may explain, summarize or assist with reasoning, but shall not silently replace validated calculations.

## 7. MODULARITY

Major components must be independently replaceable.

The architecture shall separate, where appropriate:

DATA
COMPUTATION
GIS
KNOWLEDGE
RETRIEVAL
AI
APPLICATION

Replacing one component should not require rebuilding the entire system.

## 8. OPEN-SOURCE ADOPTION

Open-source components may be adopted only after evaluation of:

- technical suitability
- scientific validity
- license compatibility
- commercial usability
- security
- maintenance
- dependency risk
- architectural compatibility

Discovery does not equal adoption.

Every important external component receives one of:

ADOPT
ADAPT
REFERENCE
REJECT

## 9. SECURITY

Security is architectural, not an afterthought.

The system shall protect:

- source code
- credentials
- proprietary datasets
- project information
- customer information
- evidence
- internal knowledge
- decision records

Secrets must never be committed to the repository.

External files and content must be treated as potentially unsafe until validated.

## 10. REGIONAL STRATEGY

The initial geographic intelligence focus is:

BALIKESİR / TÜRKİYE

Global scientific datasets may provide baseline knowledge.

Validated regional observations, measurements, field experience and completed project outcomes shall progressively create proprietary regional intelligence.

## 11. MULTI-AI GOVERNANCE

Multiple AI systems may independently:

- research
- analyze
- test
- challenge
- compare
- review

No AI has unilateral authority over the architecture or trusted core.

The preferred decision flow is:

RESEARCH
→ EVIDENCE
→ VALIDATION
→ CROSS-CHECK
→ COMPARISON
→ DECISION PROPOSAL
→ HUMAN APPROVAL
→ APPROVED KNOWLEDGE

## 12. AI ROLE PRINCIPLE

AI roles shall be capability-based, not permanently restricted to a single software layer.

An AI may challenge another AI's conclusion.

Research independence is encouraged.

Shared evidence standards are mandatory.

## 13. COMMON KNOWLEDGE POOL

Research outputs shall remain separated by source until validated.

The repository shall distinguish between:

RAW RESEARCH
→ VALIDATED EVIDENCE
→ DECISION PROPOSALS
→ APPROVED KNOWLEDGE

Unverified research must never be silently promoted to trusted knowledge.

## 14. CHANGE CONTROL

Important architectural changes require a documented decision.

Changes to foundational architecture shall include:

- reason
- alternatives considered
- evidence
- risks
- expected benefit
- approval status

No irreversible architectural change should be made solely from an AI-generated suggestion.

## 15. HUMAN AUTHORITY

The project owner retains final authority over:

- architecture
- intellectual property
- production deployment
- financial commitments
- security-sensitive changes
- irreversible decisions

AI systems assist and execute within defined permissions.

## 16. NON-NEGOTIABLE RULES

1. Evidence before assertion.
2. Structured data before semantic reasoning.
3. Deterministic computation before LLM calculation.
4. External information is untrusted until validated.
5. AI models are replaceable.
6. Important knowledge requires provenance.
7. Licenses must be verified before adoption.
8. Security is part of architecture.
9. No AI has unilateral authority.
10. Regional knowledge is a strategic asset.
11. Modules should remain replaceable.
12. Proprietary value should remain under project control.
13. Research and trusted knowledge must remain distinguishable.
14. Human approval is required for foundational decisions.

## 17. FOUNDATION STATUS

This document establishes the initial architectural constitution of PAI-FORGE.

Future documents, AI instructions, research tasks and technical decisions shall be compatible with this constitution unless a formally documented architectural revision is approved.
